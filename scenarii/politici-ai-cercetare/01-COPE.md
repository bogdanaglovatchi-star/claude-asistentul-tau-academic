# COPE — Authorship and AI tools (position statement)

> Sursă reală. COPE = Committee on Publication Ethics. Documentul original, tipărit din pagina COPE,
> e în [`documente-originale/01-COPE-position-authorship-and-AI-tools.pdf`](documente-originale/01-COPE-position-authorship-and-AI-tools.pdf).
> Verificat cuvânt cu cuvânt față de pagina live pe 8 septembrie 2026, textul e neschimbat.
> Revizuit ultima dată de COPE pe 13 februarie 2023, versiunea 1 publicată pe 20 august 2024.

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools such as ChatGPT or Large Language Models in research
publications is expanding rapidly. COPE joins organisations, such as WAME and the JAMA Network among others,
to state that **AI tools cannot be listed as an author of a paper**.

AI tools cannot meet the requirements for authorship as they **cannot take responsibility for the submitted
work**. As non-legal entities, they cannot assert the presence or absence of conflicts of interest nor manage
copyright and license agreements.

Authors who use AI tools in the writing of a manuscript, production of images or graphical elements of the
paper, or in the collection and analysis of data, **must be transparent in disclosing in the Materials and
Methods (or similar section) of the paper how the AI tool was used and which tool was used**. Authors are
**fully responsible for the content of their manuscript**, even those parts produced by an AI tool, and are
thus liable for any breach of publication ethics.
